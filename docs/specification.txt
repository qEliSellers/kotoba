0000 PRODUCT / ENGINEERING BRIEF
0001 Japanese Study Website
0002 Codex-ready functional specification  |  Revision 1.0
0003 A desktop-first, locally stored Japanese vocabulary, kanji, and verb-conjugation trainer inspired by Benkyō. This document defines the intended behavior, data structures, review algorithm, screen patterns, edge cases, and implementation tests.
0004 SOURCE BASIS
0005 Adapted from the supplied “Website idea (Benkyō recreation sorta kinda idk)” document and the subsequent decisions in the conversation. Benkyō screenshots below are references for interaction patterns, not a request to copy its visuals or proprietary code.
0006 DESIGN PRIORITIES
0007 Fast active recall; typed vocabulary/kanji answers and minimal extra clicks.
0008 Frequency-ranked, user-imported dictionaries; library entries are not automatically active flashcards.
0009 A single unified SRS history for every graded review of a given card, regardless of quiz entry point.
0010 Mixed-class verb conjugation exercises; never reveal godan/ichidan status as a hint.
0011 Desktop web only; local persistence, offline operation, and portable backups.
0012 Status: ready to implement. SRS constants are explicit provisional starting values and must remain adjustable; they are not presented as Benkyō’s internal formula.

0030 1. Scope and product rules
0031 1.1 In scope
0032 Separate vocabulary and kanji libraries with CSV imports, search, editing, and active-deck management.
0033 Independent vocabulary (two directions) and kanji (three skills) flashcards.
0034 FlexSRS-inspired urgency graph, custom/smart quiz selection, correct/incorrect/pass grading, and complete history.
0035 Separate Conjugation area with dynamically selected verbs, fixed review-slot identities, and an extensive unlockable conjugation catalog.
0036 Optional verb classification drill and fully automatic confusable-word drills.
0037 Statistics modeled on the reference screens, including per-card speed-vs-date history.
0038 Desktop-first dark-friendly layout, automatic local saving, data export/import, and recovery.
0039 1.2 Explicitly excluded from version 1
0040 No dedicated kana library or drills; no handwriting input or stroke-order testing; no mandatory listening/transcription activity; no sentence-translation flashcards or sentence mining; no vocabulary-coverage dashboard; no pitch-accent database or scoring; no AI tutor; no login or mandatory cloud service. Do not reproduce the App Store copy, subscription flow, or iOS navigation exactly.

0130 3.2 Import and edit behavior
0131 Bulk import from CSV; validate required columns, malformed ranks, missing readings, duplicate IDs, and encoding (UTF-8). Preview a count of accepted, skipped !!! rows, duplicates, and invalid rows before commit.
0132 Offer a downloadable single-entry template and an Add Entry form that uses the same schema. Enable editing and deletion in the library.
0133 Keep a stable internal identifier separate from display spelling and frequency rank. Reimports of the same source ID update the existing entry without resetting review history or duplicating deck cards.
0134 Do not automatically activate all imported words. Support selecting individual words, range/frequency selection, and bulk Add to Vocabulary Deck.
0135 Never silently delete studied cards because a later import omits their source row or marks a row excluded. Show a conflict/skip report; let the user decide explicitly.
0136 Do not conflate homographs that have different source IDs, kana readings, senses, or part-of-speech tags.
0137 Search both Japanese spelling and reading, romaji, English meanings, and frequency. Library view should expose meaning, reading, rank, active state, and edit controls.
0138 3.3 Flashcards and acceptable answers
0139 Automatic creation  Adding one vocabulary entry generates two independent cards: JP → EN and EN → JP, each with its own SRS history and due date.
0140 JP → EN  Display the Japanese word with furigana by default (an optional user toggle may hide it). Accept the primary translation and standalone meaningful alternatives in extended (split by semicolons), allowing case and punctuation normalization. Never automatically accept loosely related senses based on AI guesses.
0141 EN → JP  Display the primary English translation; require the kana pronunciation written in romaji. Accept common deterministic romaji variants, but never demand manual Japanese IME conversion or kanji characters.
0142 TTS  Automatically read the Japanese text when the Japanese side appears, including upon answer reveal (not the English gloss); provide repeat and mute settings. If speech audio is unavailable offline, do not block reviews.
0143 Notes  Show notes and other meanings on the answer/review detail, not as advance hints unless the quiz explicitly permits them.

0146 4. Kanji library and three-card model
0147 4.1 CSV fields and library
0148 Separate kanji CSV schema: id | kanji | onyomi | kunyomi | meaning | extended meaning | notes. The eventual kanji file is not finalized; implement a tolerant importer and a template with these canonical fields. Frequency ranking is optional additional metadata, not a required original field.
0149 Three-card model  For a kanji with relevant readings, create Kanji → on’yomi, Kanji → kun’yomi, and Kanji → meaning as distinct identities, not bidirectional cards. Answers are typed, with readings entered in romaji.
0150 Multiple answers  Support multiple correct on/kun readings and meaning alternatives. Only use readings explicitly supplied and designated acceptable by the imported data. Do not force the learner to recall an exhaustive dictionary of obscure readings.
0151 Missing readings  If no usable on’yomi or kun’yomi is present, do not generate an unanswerable card. Display the excluded card type in the editor with a reason. Keep the other valid kanji card(s).
0152 Kanji sourcing  Enable (a) add kanji of a particular vocabulary word, (b) add all kanji appearing in the active vocabulary deck, and (c) add top N not-yet-active eligible kanji. Only activate symbols actually present in the imported Kanji Library; report misses without inventing entries.
0153 Frequency default  If kanji CSV lacks an explicit frequency rank, derive the “top N” order from occurrences in the ranked vocabulary library: best (lowest) rank containing that kanji, then stable tie-break. Make the derived nature visible. Do not confuse this with an externally verified kanji-frequency dataset.

0158 5. Review engine, urgency graph, and scheduling
0159 5.1 Non-negotiable review principles
0160 Exactly one scheduling state per card identity. Normal, custom, smart, and targeted reviews of that same card update that state; there is no special “custom practice does not count” rule.
0161 Urgency is a HARD 0–200% bound. Cards at 200% stay at 200% for both displayed urgency and scheduling priority; do not secretly rank them above the cap by elapsed lateness.
0162 Premature reviews have a proportionately small impact. A review at 20% urgency must not reschedule a card nearly as much as a normal due review.
0163 Current correct streak and lifetime accuracy should contribute about equally to card strength; FSRS-style learned memory stability slightly less (initial weights: 35 / 35 / 30).
0164 Response speed has no penalty at or below 10 seconds. Above 10 seconds it should increasingly and aggressively limit the reward from an otherwise correct response.
0165 Conservative maturation: approximate 2 days at first correct, 2.5 days at streak 2 under ordinary urgency, and 14 days near streak 8; these are approximate empirical targets, not exact Benkyō formulas.
0166 5.2 Urgency and FlexSRS queue
0167 For ordinary scheduled cards, let t = time since last graded review and I = that card’s current scheduled interval (same units). Urgency U = clamp(100 × t / I, 0, 200). At initial learning, define U = 100 until the first graded response. Values 0–99 are early, 100 is due, 101–199 overdue, and 200 is the absolute maximum. No hidden >200 priority score.
0168 Sort cards by displayed capped urgency descending. At equal capped urgency, use a stable non-lateness tie-break (for example, current card strength then immutable ID); do not use how many days past the 200% cap a card has been. Show up to the first 300 entries in the graph by default, allowing expansion and filtering.
0169 The graph is a sequence of vertical card bars, left-to-right highest to lowest urgency, with height on a 0–200% scale and a horizontal line at 100%. User may click the Nth bar or use +/- controls to select exactly the first N; selected bars turn yellow. Start Review queues those cards. Allow early cards if the user manually includes them. All modes use the same urgency/scheduling rules.

0174 5.3 Configurable first-pass scheduler — explicit implementation model
0175 The following is an IMPLEMENTATION PROPOSAL, not a verified reverse engineering of Benkyō. All constants should live in one documented configuration object and be unit-tested. Record the inputs and outputs of each interval calculation for debugging.
0176 Baseline  Let s = new consecutive-correct streak. For s >= 1, use B(s) = 2 + 0.5 × (s − 1)^1.633 days. This passes through (1, 2d), (2, 2.5d), and approximately (8, 14d). At large streaks, cap the practical maximum interval to a configurable ceiling (default 365 days).
0177 Accuracy  Keep raw lifetime correct/total and a Bayesian-smoothed accuracy estimate using a small configurable prior. A 1/1 card must not be considered as proven as a 50/50 card.
0178 Memory  Maintain an FSRS-inspired difficulty/stability state or integrate a well-tested FSRS library for memory estimation. Do not simply use unmodified FSRS due dates as the final schedule; its contribution to card strength is 30% initially.
0179 Strength  Normalize streak consistency, smoothed lifetime accuracy, and memory stability into 0–1 scores. Composite strength Q = 0.35 × streak_score + 0.35 × accuracy_score + 0.30 × memory_score. Avoid double-counting the raw streak so strongly that accuracy becomes negligible; tune the reference strength multiplier by streak.
0180 Normal correctness  Compute a candidate interval from B(s) × a bounded strength adjustment (initial suggested range 0.75–1.25). At U=100 and typical speed/strength, it should match the calibration anchors. A correct response at 200% can earn an overdue bonus no greater than 2× the candidate interval; values beyond 200% provide no bonus.
0181 Early answers  For U<100, let update_weight = (U/100)^2. Blend the candidate next-due date with the existing next-due date by this weight. Example: at U=20, only 4% of the due-date adjustment applies. This is the same rule in every quiz, not a separate custom-quiz exception.
0182 Incorrect or Pass  Record the failure, reset the current correct streak, reduce memory stability, and move the next due date substantially earlier when the card is due. Preserve lifetime history so a well-established card can rebuild faster than an unfamiliar card. Starting default: 1-day relearning for a due failed card; expose the setback and recovery as adjustable parameters. For early failures, the capped urgency-scaled update weight still matters, but the streak is always reset.
0183 Speed  On a Correct response: no penalty for t ≤ 10s. Suggested earned-growth multipliers: 12s → 0.8, 15s → 0.5, 20s → 0.2, ≥25s → 0.05, interpolated between points. A slow correct remains “correct” and adds to lifetime correct count, but may make little scheduling progress. Stop timing at submission or reveal (before self-grading).
0184 Order of operations  Log raw response and time → update correct/incorrect counters and streak → update memory state → calculate quality/penalty → calculate candidate next interval → apply capped lateness factor and early-review blending → save next_due plus a transparent debug record.
0185 The first correct response to a new card should produce a two-day interval in the normal case. The observed “second correct at 200% → five days” is compatible with a 2× capped lateness bonus, but that relationship remains a testable approximation, not a proven rule. Do not treat these calibration values as fixed across every card.

0218 6. Verb conjugation drills and generator
0219 6.1 Core behavior
0220 Conjugation is a dedicated navigation area and an independent deck. Every listed form starts locked/disabled. Enable individual forms to generate review slots.
0221 Each enabled form has 50 stable numbered slots (#1…#50), and each slot has two independently scheduled directions, for a maximum of 100 card identities per form.
0222 A stable slot is an abstract skill, NOT a particular verb. Reassign its example verb at the beginning of each quiz/session; never change that verb in the middle of a quiz. Save the actual verb in the review history.
0223 Only verbs ACTIVE in the Vocabulary Deck may appear, never library-only verbs. Use metadata to distinguish godan, ichidan, する, and 来る internally, but NEVER expose the class to the learner during conjugation questions.
0224 Choose verbs with frequency-weighted randomness without replacement within each conjugation form for that quiz. A verb may recur in a different form. If fewer than 50 eligible distinct verbs exist, use only the available number of filled slots.
0225 Reserve slot #3 for する and #4 for 来る whenever those verbs are active and linguistically applicable. Do not inject absent verbs into the active vocabulary deck; disable the reservation if the verb is ineligible.
0226 Direction A: show dictionary form + short conjugation label; user types the conjugated Japanese pronunciation in romaji. Direction B: show a conjugated Japanese form; user recalls dictionary form + label, reveals the answer, and self-grades Correct or Pass/Incorrect.
0227 Prompt labels should remain quickly readable, including “Present” (rather than forcing “Non-past”). Do not add verb-class hints or grammatical lectures to quiz prompts.
0228 6.2 Generator correctness
0229 Implement deterministic rule-based conjugation using verb-class metadata. Handle godan final-consonant categories, ichidan stems, する, 来る, irregular 行く (行って/行った), ある (negative ない), and applicable irregular or lexical exceptions. Do not generate answers using unvalidated free-form LLM output. Validate outputs against a curated test table per form and representative verb class.
0230 Some forms are semantically unnatural for particular verbs. The engine must provide an eligibility flag per (verb, form) so such examples can be excluded, not blindly generated. Causative-passive contracted forms such as 書かされる may be accepted as valid alternatives where linguistically appropriate. Include common polite-desire alternatives where valid, but do not accept semantically distinct ないで and なくて as identical.

0246 7. Classification and automatic confusable-word practice
0247 7.1 Verb classification
0248 Optional quick drill under Study: select a verb from the active vocabulary deck, show its dictionary form, kana, and optionally a brief gloss, then present exactly three instant-response buttons: Godan / Ichidan / Irregular. After answering, show the correct class and optionally the conjugated example. Allow an optional “tricky る-ending verbs” selection. Do not reveal the class in the separate Conjugation quizzes.
0249 7.2 Confusable vocabulary: automatic generation only
0250 The user MUST NOT be expected to curate lists of confusing words. Produce optional contrast sets automatically using (in priority order): (a) evidence from actual typed wrong answers that match another active vocabulary card’s valid pronunciation or meaning; (b) kana/romaji pronunciation similarity and identical readings; and (c) overlapping English meanings, using only imported data. Do not use pitch-accent distinctions or add pitch-accent metadata.
0251 Wrong-answer associations  For an incorrectly answered typed vocabulary card, normalize the wrong response and see whether it matches the expected Japanese reading or English meaning of another ACTIVE vocabulary entry. Increase a directed confusion-pair score, weighted by repeats and recency. Ambiguous one-word glosses should produce low-confidence candidates, not asserted confusion facts.
0252 Phonetic similarity  Compare kana strings after normalization using simple edit distance and phonetic-family rules; identical readings (帰る, 変える, 買える) are strong candidates for visual/meaning-based discrimination, not audio-only tests.
0253 Semantic similarity  Compare existing translations/extended meanings and identify overlapping word senses. Favor words the learner has actually reviewed; use semantics only to propose candidates, not to invent translations.
0254 Quiz mode  Use 3- or 4-option multiple-choice contrasts only within this feature. Show a meaningful prompt (e.g., “Which means return home?”), select candidates whose imported senses give an unambiguous right answer, and display a succinct comparison afterward. All alternatives MUST be from the active vocab deck.
0255 Scheduling  Treat a confusable quiz that directly tests an existing vocabulary direction as a graded review of that SAME vocab card with the normal urgency-sensitive scheduler; do not create duplicate vocabulary SRS states. Record its choice-based input type for later analysis. If the question tests an abstract contrast rather than one definable card, record it in drill statistics without inventing a card identity.
0256 Workload  Confusables remain an optional Study shortcut. Never force a new required queue or overwhelm normal daily reviews.

0271 9. Persistence, quality, and implementation milestones
0272 9.1 Local storage and recoverability
0273 The site must work without a mandatory external account. Use a local database (for example, SQLite with a lightweight local server, or an appropriate durable browser database). Persist every library edit, card activation, quiz result, and SRS update transactionally. No data-loss on restart. Provide Export All / Import Backup to portable human-inspectable structured files, with version metadata and tested round-trips.
0274 Avoid destructive schema upgrades. For source rows or cards that are deleted by the user, require confirmation, protect historical audit records unless the user explicitly deletes those too, and save a pre-migration backup before upgrades.

0467 Appendix B. Acceptance tests and defaults
0468 Codex should write automated tests for the following behaviors, not merely demonstrate them with screenshots.
0469 Area
0470 Pass condition
0471 Import
0472 A CSV with 250 records, some !!! notes, invalid rows, and duplicate IDs previews exact results. Excluded rows never create cards.
0473 Persistence
0474 Add a vocab item and study it, exit/restart the app, verify library, review history, interval, and next due persist exactly.
0475 Vocab generation
0476 Adding 食べる creates exactly two independent card IDs; each has a separate streak, answers, and next due.
0477 Synonyms
0478 JP→EN まさか accepts “no way”, “surely not”, and “unexpected” from its extended list; capitalization/punctuation differences do not matter.
0479 Romaji
0480 EN→JP 食べる accepts correctly normalized romaji, not only Japanese keyboard output.
0481 Kanji generation
0482 An imported kanji with on’yomi, kun’yomi, and meaning yields three cards; missing-type readings do not yield an impossible question.
0483 Kanji extraction
0484 Adding kanji from active vocabulary ignores symbols missing in the Kanji Library and reports their count.
0485 Urgency cap
0486 For a ten-day interval: at day 10 urgency=100, day 20=200, day 100=200. No late bonus or priority above 200.
0487 Queue selection
0488 Clicking bar #54 highlights exactly the top 54 ranked cards; Start Review contains those identities and no others.
0489 Early review
0490 At urgency 20%, a correct response changes next_due minimally compared with an otherwise identical 100% review.
0491 Scheduling anchors
0492 New-card first correct at normal urgency ~2d; second correct at 100% ~2.5d; streak 8 under reference conditions ~14d; at 200% no more than 2x late boost.
0493 Speed
0494 Correct answers at 9.9s and 10.0s have no speed penalty; 15s receives a strong penalty; ≥25s almost no positive interval-growth credit.
0495 Unified history
0496 Review a vocab card through custom quiz, then FlexSRS; both attempts appear under one card and update one scheduling state.
0497 Conjugation identity
0498 Same slot changes from 飲む one session to 考える in another without creating a new SRS history.
0499 Mixed classification
0500 Conjugation questions do not show godan/ichidan/irregular labels; a standalone three-button classification exercise does.
0501 Irregular verbs
0502 Test する, 来る, 行く, ある, multiple godan endings, ichidan, and applicable causative-passive contractions.
0503 Conjugation selection
0504 No verb outside active Vocabulary Deck is used; no duplicate verb for the same form in one quiz; slots #3 and #4 are reserved when eligible.
0505 Auto confusables
0506 Incorrect typed answer matching another active vocab entry increases a confusion-pair score; optional contrast drill uses plausible unique-answer distractors.
0507 Stats
0508 Per-card speed/date plot has one dot per graded review with the proper color, plus raw timestamp/answer/urgency/interval row.
0509 Backup
0510 Export, erase test installation, reimport, verify deterministic round-trip for entries, cards, attempts, and schedules.
0512 Defaults codified without additional user approval
0513 The initial storage implementation is local; no cloud account. Backup/export is user-triggered and repeatable.
0514 All conjugation forms begin disabled. The active verb inventory is the sole source of generated conjugation examples.
0515 Maximum 50 conjugation slots/form/direction; no padded repeated verbs when fewer than 50 eligible words exist.
0516 A new card starts at urgency 100; a card’s due-state formula applies after its first graded attempt.
